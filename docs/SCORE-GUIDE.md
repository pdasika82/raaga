# Reading your score

Every saved session gets a number from 0 to 100 in a coloured circle. It combines two
measurements of your pitch:

- **Tuning (70% of the score).** How close each moment of singing was to the nearest
  note of the scale. Time within ±20 cents earns full credit, time within ±50 cents
  earns partial credit, anything further earns nothing.
- **Scale adherence (30% of the score).** The share of singing time spent on notes
  that belong to the lesson's scale or raga. This is the "On scale tones" line under
  the score.

| Score | Colour | What it usually means | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 80 and above | green | Notes landed and stayed in tune | Polish steadiness, then raise the tempo |
| 55 to 79 | yellow | Right notes mostly, but often 20 to 50 cents off, or a few strays outside the scale | Slow down and hold each note until its pill turns green |
| below 55 | orange | Many notes never settled, or a good share were outside the scale | Halve the tempo; check Sa with Find my Sa; hum each note before singing it |

## The lines under the score

- **Within ±20 cents / ±50 cents**: share of singing time that close to a scale note.
  100 cents is one half-step, so 50 cents is halfway to the next note.
- **On scale tones**: share of time on an allowed note at all.
- **Average deviation**: mean distance from the nearest scale note, in cents. Under
  15 is good, over 35 means notes were landing between pitches.
- **Steadiness on held notes**: how much a held note wobbled. Under 8 cents is steady.
- **Range**: lowest and highest notes you held long enough to count.

## What the score does not measure

Only pitch is measured. Tone, breath, rhythm, diction and ornamentation are invisible
to it, and it does not know whether you sang the notes in the order the lesson asked.
The coach feedback reads the note sequence alongside the lesson text and covers that.

## Comparing scores

- Compare the same lesson at the same tempo. A fast, slurred run scores low even when
  the notes are right, because each note never gets time to settle.
- Look at the trend across a week rather than one attempt.
- Do not chase 100. A steady 85 on a slow scale, then the same at full speed, is real
  progress.
